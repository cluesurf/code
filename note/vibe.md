# A base is a vibe

**The central claim of the whole project.** A base concept is not a verb and
not a noun. It is a generalized experience, and it is called a **vibe**.

Everything else follows from this, including why the base list looks nothing
like a dictionary.

## The problem with English

Take `discuss`.

In English that word arrives already shaped. It is a verb. It comes with a
subject who does it, an object it is done to, a tense, and the whole apparatus
of verbhood. To get at the thing itself you have to strip all of that off, and
English gives you no way to write down what is left.

So English makes you pick a lump and then bolts more lumps onto it:

```
discuss        the verb
discussion     the verb plus a noun ending
discussant     the verb plus a person ending
discursive     a different stem entirely
```

**The thing all four share has no name in English.** There is no word for the
bare experience of discussing, untensed, unpersoned, neither act nor object.
The closest English gets is the verb, and the verb is already a commitment.

That is the mental baggage. Every English root drags a part of speech behind
it, and you cannot put the root down without the baggage.

## What Code does instead

**The base is the bare experience. The class is added separately.**

```
<vibe>              the generalized experience, no class at all
<vibe> <class>      the same experience, said as a verb or a noun
<vibe> <kind>       the same experience, narrowed to a kind of thing
```

Each of those is four letters, so a verb is eight letters and a noun is eight
letters and neither is privileged.

```
mmmm            experience
mmmm mmzz       to experience
mmmm mmss       an experience
mmmm mmww       experiencingly
mmmm mmdd       experiential
```

`mmmm` on its own is none of those. It is the vibe.

## The five classes

They add no meaning. That is the whole point, and it is what separates them
from kinds.

```
mmzz  action   says the vibe as a doing            Tune -i
mmss  object   says the vibe as a thing            Tune -a
mmww  manner   says the vibe as a way of doing     Tune -e
mmdd  design   says the vibe as a feature          Tune -o
mmkk  cradle   says the vibe as an orientation     Tune -u
```

**Tune does the same job with a vowel**, because Tune has to be short enough
to say out loud. Code has no such constraint and pays four letters for
perfect uniformity. Same five classes, same five jobs, different budget.

## Where the kinds come in

This is where the `discussion = discuss + result` work pays off.

Once the base is a bare vibe, the question "what kind of thing is the noun"
becomes a real question with a real answer, and there are 132 answers. The
132 kinds are the inventory of what you can append.

```
<discuss vibe> mndn       the process of it
<discuss vibe> mndd       one occurrence of it
<discuss vibe> mndb       what it leaves behind
<discuss vibe> mddm       one who does it
<discuss vibe> mddh       the method of it
```

**English writes all five of those as `discussion` or `discussant` and makes
the reader work out which.** That is not a flaw in the reader. There is
genuinely nothing in the word that says.

The full argument, with every common English ending broken down, is in
Tune's own write-up of the last word. The short version: no common English suffix
carries fewer than eight distinct concepts, and `-ment` and `-ing` carry
fourteen each.

## Why this is not just tidiness

**The class is a choice, and English hides the choice inside the root.**

When a language gives you `discuss` as a verb and `discussion` as a noun, it
has already decided that discussing is primarily a doing and secondarily a
thing. For `discuss` that seems harmless. For other words it is not.

```
thought    is it the act of thinking or the thing thought
work       is it the doing or the product
love       is it a feeling, an act, or a relation
fear       the same
```

English answers differently for each, inconsistently, and the answer is baked
into which form got the short spelling. A vibe has no such bias. `mmmm` is
not secretly a noun.

## What a vibe is not

**Not a verb, even when the English gloss is a verb.** `fill` is not "to
fill". It is the vibe of receptive pulling, of gathering inward. Read as an
action it is filling, read as an object it is a fullness, read as a feature
it is receptive. None of the three is the base.

**Not vague.** A vibe is precise about *what* and silent about *how it is
grammatically used*. Those are different things. `mnmn` is exactly the felt
value axis of pleasure, pain and peace, and says nothing about whether you
are naming it, doing it, or describing with it.

**Not a word.** A word is a vibe plus a class, at minimum. The base list is
not a word list, and asking a base which part of speech it is has the same
shape as asking a number what colour it is.

## What this costs

**Everything is longer.** Eight letters to say a verb, where English says
seven for `discuss`. Code buys uniformity with length, and length is the
cheap resource when a machine is reading.

**The gloss column lies a little.** Every base has an English note, and every
English note is written in some part of speech, because English has no other
mode. `the receptive pull of a link` reads as a noun phrase. That is the
gloss, not the base. There is no way to write a vibe in English and this is
a known and permanent limitation of the notes.

## The lineage

This is Tune's model, carried over unchanged. Tune calls base words
**spirits**, and says the same thing:

> everything begins with spirits or base words, and we add one of the 5
> possible endings to each base word to turn it into one of the 5 types

The only difference is the budget. Tune spends a vowel, Code spends four
letters.
